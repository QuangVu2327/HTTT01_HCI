###### **Looking at reading** 



- Stages in the reading process 

   - (1) Perceive visual _pattern of a word_ on the page 

   - – (2) Decode with reference to an internal representation of _language_ 

   - (3) Process _syntactic/semantic analysis_ and operate on phrases or sentences 

- (1) & (2) influence interface design 

   - Text legibility : reading speed 

   - Design of textual interfaces carefully 

      - Word shape, font size, spacing between lines, line length

###### **Reasoning** 



- Problem 

   - If _an event always follows an action_ , the user will infer that _the event is caused by the action_ unless evidence to the contrary is made available 

   - In fact, if the _event_ and the _action_ are unrelated, confusion and even error often result 

- Eg. 

   - An interaction design flaw that traps user reasoning when they click the 'Place Order / Checkout' button

###### **Norman’s model** 



- The most influential in HCI 

   - Close to our intuitive understanding of the interaction between human and computer 

###### • Idea 

- User formulates a plan of action, which is then executed at the computer interface 

- When the plan, or part of the plan, has been **_executed_** , the user observes the computer interface to **_evaluate_** the result of the executed plan, and to determine further actions 

82 

HCI - FIT - HCMUS 

###### **7 stages in Norman’s model** 



- (1) Establishing the goal 

- (2) Forming the intention 

- (3) Specifying the action sequence 

- (4) Executing the action 

- (5) Perceiving the system state 

- (6) Interpreting the system state 

- (7) Evaluating the system state with respect to the goals and intentions 

83 

HCI - FIT - HCMUS

###### **Example** 



- You are sitting reading as evening falls – Need more light 

- Establish the goal to get more light 

- Form an intention to switch on the desk lamp 

- Specify the actions required, to reach over and press the lamp switch 

- If someone else is closer the intention may be different 

   - May ask them to switch on the light for you 

   - _Goal is the same but the intention and actions are different_ 

- If the light does not come on 

   - Indicate the bulb has blown or the lamp is not plugged into the mains 

   - – _Will formulate new goals to deal with this_ 

- If the light does come on 

   - Evaluate the new state according to the original goals 

      - Is there now enough light? 

      - _If so, the cycle is complete_ 

      - If not, formulate a new intention to switch on the main ceiling light

###### **Human error** 



###### • Slips 

   - You understand a system well, you may know exactly what to do to satisfy your goals 

   - Eg. you have formulated the correct action, but fail to execute that action correctly 

      - Mistype or accidentally press the mouse button at the wrong time 

   - Correct by better screen design 

- Mistakes 

   - You don’t know the system well, you may not even formulate the right goal 

   - Eg. you think that the magnifying glass icon is the ‘find’ function, but in fact it is to magnify the text 

   - Correct by better understanding of the system 

      - More radical redesign, improved training, a totally different metaphor for use
